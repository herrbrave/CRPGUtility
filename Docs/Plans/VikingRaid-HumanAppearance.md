# NPC appearance creator, family 3: Sidekick humans (the Viking kitbash)

Status: DESIGN v2 (2026-10-06), Opus-reviewed (Section 8 supersedes Sections 3-5 where they differ); Phase A BUILT (Section 9). Builds on the Viking family (`VikingRaid-NpcAppearance.md` Sections 12-17) and the goblin family (`VikingRaid-GoblinAppearance.md`). Decision by the user: kitbash (Starter knight and civilian clothes + the Polygon Viking hair/beards/helmets/capes/pouches) and MODIFY existing meshes where they do not fit, rather than hand-modelling clothing. The Sidekick humans are the base because every Viking body has a full beard baked into its mesh (all 10, female too, not removable; Section 13 of the Viking doc).

## 1. Goal

A human NPC is authored as data (`NpcDefinition.appearance`, saved with the character), dressed at spawn from one shared base prefab, edited with `< Title: Value >` rows on a few tabs and a live preview in the `NpcDefinition` inspector: the same machinery as the Viking and goblin families, a third catalog and base prefab. The look: a clean-shaven Sidekick human in earthy Starter clothes with Polygon Viking helmets, hair, beards, capes and pouches on top.

## 2. Facts (verified in the spikes; details in Viking doc Sections 14-17)

- Sidekick human parts: one base body (TORS AUPL/R ALWL/R HNDL/R HIPS LEGL/R FOTL/R), HEAD x2, EYEL/R, TONG, and 10 species variants of HAIR, EBRL/R, EARL/R, NOSE, TETH, FCHR (facial hair). Part sets bind to the skeleton-only master by bone name (the goblin machinery: bind bones, rest poses from the master asset, dynamic chains). 4 body shape keys on each body part.
- Starter outfits (own meshes per region, all 12 body regions incl. hands, plus attachments ASHL/R AHPB/F/L/R AKNL/R AEBL/R ABAC AFAC AHED): `SK_FANT_KNGT_17` (knight), `SK_SCFI_CIVL_09` (civilian, also one HAIR), `SK_SCFI_CIVL_10` (accessories only), `SK_HORR_VILN_01` (a head piece). With their own Starter colour maps both read as detailed fantasy adventurer clothing (`appearance_human_starter_outfits.png`); colour maps 1-4 recolour them, but the map carries SKIN, HAIR and clothing together (hair is blue in maps 2-4).
- Polygon pieces fit a Sidekick human with the Polygon mount rule (local position zero, rotation = the inverse of the bone's REST rotation read from the master asset, one uniform scale 0.935 = Sidekick height / Polygon height; bones `head`, `spine_03`, `pelvis`): static hair (Viking 06-09, Generic 04-11 + bun + ponytail, War Camp 01-07), helmets, horns, wings, crowns, hats, eyepatch, masks, goggles, capes, backpacks, bags, pouches, bottles, collar, beards and moustaches (Viking 5, War Camp 2, Generic 2, chops, moustache). Skinned Polygon pieces fit after `BakeMesh` into rigid meshes: the 8 Viking braided/styled hairs and the wolf pelt. Shoulder furs need fitting; the bear pelt and Viking Crown_01 render cyan (material).
- The Blender tunic (`Blender/SidekickVikingOutfit/`) is a plain peasant garment (flat colour cells), works through the applier, but is a "second skin" (Section 17).
- The human faces -Z after the UAL hero idle (cause unconfirmed: the clip, not the human; the goblin faces the camera with its own clips).

## 4. Changes to the existing system

1. **Catalog**: no new kinds needed. Polygon pieces are `AttachStatic` options (`bone`, `localEuler` = the inverse of the master asset's rest rotation of that bone, `localScale` = 0.935, computed by the author), Sidekick pieces and outfits are `AttachParts`. New catalog field `previewYaw`.
2. **Applier** (`ModelAppearancePart`): a `Repaints` flag (true for `AttachParts` parts, false for the others). With the human catalog's `AppearanceParts` palette scope, ONLY `Repaints` parts take the colour map; the Polygon pieces keep their own materials (today every marked part would be repainted). The Viking (`FamilyMaterials`) and goblin (all `AttachParts`) behaviour is unchanged.
3. **Author-time bake** (`PolygonPieceBaker`, Editor): for each skinned Polygon piece wanted (8 hairs, wolf pelt): activate it on the Polygon master at rest, `BakeMesh`, transform the vertices and normals into the frame of the Polygon `Head` bone (pivot at the bone, upright world axes, no scale), save a Mesh asset and a one-renderer prefab (Polygon material) under `Assets/Appearance/SidekickHumans/Baked/` (derived Synty geometry: ignored by `.gitignore`).
4. **`HumanCatalogAuthor`** (`VikingRaid.Appearance.Editor`): `HumanMaster.prefab` (skeleton-only, from HumanSpecies_01, dynamic chains stripped as for the goblin), the catalog asset, ids, defaults, throws listing every missing file. Rows as in Section 3; the Blender tunic parts are optional only if the Blender output exists (documented), else the author fails loudly.
5. **Base prefab**: `HumanNpcBase.prefab` through `CharacterRosterAuthor` (`Entry`: hero animation profile, the Human catalog, measured yaw, baked default look), outside the combat roster folder. Family registry gains `("Human", ".../HumanNpcBase.prefab")`. The inspector, creator model, preview and family-switch pruning are already family-neutral.
6. **Facing**: find the cause (sample the goblin idle on the human; check `CharacterPlayableAnimator` in game) and store ONE constant read by both the preview and the runtime. Not `ModelYawCorrection` (it returns to 0 during actions).
7. **Blender**: fix the tunic seams (taper the offset to 0 at the wrist ring and the sleeve end), collapse to ONE material before export, verify the 4 shape names resolve in Unity.

## 5. Tests (tests first, real Unity runs)

EditMode: catalog structure (10 rows, 4 tabs, required rows have defaults, unique ids, every file/prefab exists); the human master is skeleton-only; every outfit applies with no warning and gives exactly one renderer per body region; EVERY Build weight on EVERY outfit resolves the shape names and leaves no residual; boundary vertices of adjacent regions coincide (<= 1 mm) at rest and at each Build for each outfit (the seam test); Polygon pieces upright for the character (their forward agrees with the character's after the idle is sampled), keep their own materials when a Look is chosen, and clones are stripped; `facialhair` is one row holding Sidekick and Polygon options; the randomizer never gives a beard on a Feminine build unless asked; the three families coexist in the inspector (family switch pruning with the human ids); `BindPoseAudit` clean for the Blender parts. PlayMode: a human NPC spawned in the village is dressed, follows its idle and walk, faces the way it moves, and is rebuilt from saved state.

## 6. Risks and what is deliberately not done

- Colour: the Starter maps carry skin and clothing together; Look cannot change skin tone alone. First cut: curate which of the 4 maps look right with each outfit (map 1 is the earthy one) and accept the Sidekick hair taking the map's hair colour while Polygon hair keeps its own; later: script-generated palettes (Synty's face/skin/hair cells kept, an outfit block we own).
- Rigid Polygon accessories clip on Buff (8.6 cm) and Heavy (16 cm) bodies: a known limit until a Blender "transfer" helper (Surface Deform + shape-key bake + Data Transfer weights) turns chosen pieces into deforming parts (Phase C).
- Cost: ~12 face + 12 body + 3-5 accessories = ~30 renderers per human; `updateWhenOffscreen` on every part is wasteful (set `localBounds` from the blend-shape extent) and a mesh merge is a later, measured step.
- `masculineFeminine` range unverified (so the build row offers Feminine only as an assumption); shoulder furs, bear pelt, hoods/shawl, Crown_01 material: Phase B/C.
- Phases: A = the family without baked pieces (static Polygon pieces, Starter outfits, our tunic, tests); B = baked hairs and wolf pelt, furs fitting; C = transfer helper, relief bands, more outfits, palettes.

## 8. Review resolutions (Opus, read-only, 2026-10-06; checked against the repo, the surprising ones re-verified by me) -- these supersede Sections 3-5 where they differ

Status: DESIGN v2, approved by the user's choice of the kitbash direction; Phase A is built next.

**Facts corrected**
- Polygon capes are NOT static: they have 2-4 skinned renderers and are `AttachOwnRig` (`ModelAppearanceCatalogAuthor` classifies them); the Polygon hoods/shawl are `AttachRigged`, which needs a master MESH (`AttachRigged` returns at `MasterMeshes(root).FirstOrDefault() == null`), so on a skeleton-only master they are impossible, not deferred. Beards are static prefabs (Section 15 of the Viking doc said "after baking": wrong). NOSE has 11 variants (10 used); HEAD02 and 38WRAP exist and are deliberately unused. "Map 1 is the earthy one" is wrong for the civilian (red with a green sash).
- Starter parts carry the 4 body shape keys on every body region and on KNGT/CIVL_09 shoulders, but NOT on the hip attachments (AHP*), back, elbow, knee pieces, CIVL_09 AHED, or CIVL_10 shoulders (verified by file names). So a `belt` row would never follow a Build.
- The Starter outfit check silently fell back to the base part when an outfit file was missing, so it proved nothing about completeness; the file lists (all 12 body regions plus 22-34 for KNGT_17 and CIVL_09; CIVL_09 also 02HAIR; CIVL_10 only AHED/AHPB/L/R/ASHL/R; VILN_01 only AHED) are verified separately.
- A seam test of "boundary vertices coincide" is wrong for Starter outfits (Synty layers its garments, it does not weld them): only the Blender tunic gets a seam/gap check.
- The facing: the idle clips turn the BODY ~165 degrees on both Sidekick masters (hero UAL idle: hip yaw -166.5 on HumanMaster and GoblinMaster, 0 turn of the root; the goblin idle gives +18.5 on both); on the Polygon rig the same clip turns the model ROOT by 196.8 and the hips read -2.1 after the preview resets the root. So it is the clip's body yaw, not the human; the in-game value (root motion off, `CharacterPlayableAnimator`) must be MEASURED on the built base prefab with the `GameFacingTests.HipYaw` definition, per state (idle, moving, action).

**Decisions**
1. **Repainting is derived from the catalog, not stored.** Under the `AppearanceParts` scope `ApplyPalette` repaints a part only when its option's kind is `AttachParts` (looked up as `catalog.FindSlot(part.SlotId).Find(part.OptionId).kind`). No new serialized flag (a baked prefab, like the goblin base with its baked default parts, would deserialize a new bool as false and stop taking a Look). `AttachStatic`, `AttachOwnRig` and `AttachRigged` pieces keep their own materials. Goblin and Viking behaviour unchanged.
2. **Location**: the human master and catalog live in the UNIGNORED `Assets/Appearance/Human/` (like `Assets/Appearance/Goblin/`), so a committed `HumanNpcBase.prefab` and NPC assets do not dangle on a fresh clone; only derived geometry (the Blender tunic FBXs, later `Baked/`) stays in the ignored `Assets/Appearance/SidekickHumans/`. The spike's `SidekickHumans/HumanMaster.prefab` is retired.
3. **Rows (Phase A)**: Face tab `face` (species 1-10, required), `hair` (Sidekick HAIR 1-10 + Polygon static hair), `facialhair` (Sidekick FCHR 1-10 + Polygon beards/moustache/chops: ONE row); Outfit tab `outfit` (required, ONE whole-body row: Bare, Viking Tunic, Civilian, Knight), `shoulders` (KNGT and CIVL_09 ASHL/R); Gear tab `headwear` (Polygon helmets/horns/wings/crowns/hats), `faceware` (eyepatch, goggles, masks: its own row so an eyepatch can go with a helmet), `cloak` (Polygon capes, `AttachOwnRig`), `gear` (pouches, bags, backpacks, bottles, collar); Colour tab `look` (Starter maps 1-4), `build` (Default, Buff, Heavy, Skinny). Dropped from Phase A: `belt` (no shape keys), the Feminine build (range of `masculineFeminine` unverified, and the "no beard on a feminine build" rule needs catalog-level option exclusions that do not exist), hoods and shawls (impossible on this master), baked hairs / wolf pelt (Phase B, appended options only), furs, the Starter AHED/AFAC/ABAC/AEB/AKN attachments, CIVL_10, VILN_01 (deliberately unused, listed).
4. **Human catalog forbids `AttachRigged`** (test). The author needs its OWN rest-rotation code for `head`, `spine_03`, `pelvis` (the Viking author's is hard-wired to `Head/Spine_03/Hips` and the Viking master).
5. **Base prefab**: `CharacterRosterAuthor.Entry` with the hero animation profile, the Human catalog, an EXPLICIT measured `Yaw` (a hero entry silently gets (0, 30)), `BakeDefaultLook = true`, a menu `Viking Raid/Appearance/Author Human Base Prefab`; the Families registry gains `("Human", "Assets/Characters/Npcs/HumanNpcBase.prefab")` plus a button test. Facing: after measuring, store ONE constant on `ModelYawCorrection` (a field added in every state, never eased to zero) authored from the catalog's value that the preview also reads; never in the model's local transform (`CharacterView.SetModel` resets it) and never by flipping `IdleYaw` (actions return to 0 and the character would flip on every attack).
6. **Cross-family ids**: the Human ids for Polygon pieces deliberately reuse the Viking ids (`headwear-viking-helmet-horns-02`), so a Viking-to-Human switch KEEPS hair, headwear, cloak, gear (desirable; tested); Viking `face` (an eyepatch) is pruned (human `face` is the species row; `faceware` has other ids). `Catalogs()` only sees families whose base prefab exists: the human base prefab must exist for pruning to treat its entries as known.
7. **Tests**: mirror `GoblinAppearanceTests` (with `LogCapture`): structure, completeness, no `AttachRigged`, one outfit gives one renderer per body region, every Build weight on every outfit resolves the shape names and leaves no residual, Polygon pieces keep their own materials under a Look and are upright for the character after the idle is sampled, the Sidekick-part filter for tests that count renderers or check materials (capes add renderers), a three-family prune test, `BindPoseAudit` on the Blender and Starter parts, the tunic gap check, PlayMode human spawn + facing measured. Ids are permanent; Phase B appends options only (no retired-to-replacement map yet).
8. **Blender tunic fixes**: taper the offset to 0 at the wrist ring and the sleeve end, ONE material before export, then verify the four shape names in Unity.
9. **Phase A is independent of B and C** (hair and headwear rows simply lack the baked options); it depends on the facing measurement and the tunic fixes.

**What the build found**
1. **Facing is NOT a problem in the game.** The editor's `SampleAnimation` of the UAL idle turns a Sidekick body ~165 degrees (hip yaw -166.5 on both Sidekick masters; on the Polygon rig the same turn lands on the model ROOT, 196.8, which the preview resets), but in the running game (root motion off, `CharacterPlayableAnimator`) the human stands at -3.3 degrees and walks +28.5 degrees off its direction of travel, the same ~30 the Polygon heroes show for the UAL jog. So: the base prefab gets the measured `(idle -3.3, moving 28.5)` through the existing `ModelYawCorrection` (idle 0.0 / walking 0.1 measured afterwards); the PREVIEW undoes the sampled body turn itself (`FaceTheCamera`: it turns the model root by minus the hip-line yaw), for every family. No `previewYaw` field and no constant on `ModelYawCorrection` were needed.
2. **C# static initialisation order**: `HumanNpcBase` (a `static readonly Entry`) read `HumanYaw` as `(0, 0)` because it was declared BEFORE `HumanYaw`; the base prefab was rebuilt with no correction until the field was moved above it. (Comment in the code.)
3. **Palette repaint** is decided at paint time from the catalog (`catalog.FindSlot(part.SlotId).Find(part.OptionId).kind == AttachParts`), not from a stored flag: a Polygon helmet or cape keeps its own material under a Look (test), baked goblin prefab parts keep working.
4. Tunic gap: with the sleeve offset tapered to 0 at the wrist ring, the sleeve's last ring is within 2.5 cm of the hand (test). Shape keys resolve on every outfit part (Blender-exported names end in `.defaultBuff` etc.).
5. Cross-family ids: the Human Polygon ids equal the Viking ids, so a Viking-to-Human switch keeps hair, headwear, cloak and gear and drops the Viking body row and the eyepatch (tests); a goblin-to-human switch drops the goblin pieces of the shared rows.

## 10. Phase C3: independent skin tone and hair colour (design v2, 2026-10-08, after the Opus review)

Why: the `look` row (Starter maps 1-4) carries skin, hair AND clothing together, so a Look cannot change skin tone alone and Starter maps 2-4 force blue hair.

**Measured facts** (`PaletteCellProbe`, `VikingRaid_Logs/spikes/palette_cells.txt`; the Opus review re-verified the PNG orientation: `GetPixels32` is bottom row first, `y = 0` at the bottom). The colour map is 32x32 point-filtered flat cells. Skin (bare body and head): main cells (2,4) (4,4) (2,6) (4,6) (2,8) (4,8) (Starter_01 `#BB8B67`), lighter cells (0,10) (0,11) (2,10) (4,10) AND the nose (2,2) (`#D5A57B`). NOT skin: underwear (0,18) (0,19) (1,19) (clothing), lips/tongue (4,2) (6,2) (6,6), eyes and teeth (they keep following the look: irises and teeth still differ between looks, and the tunic samples the iris cell (8,4); a later `eyecolour` row is out of scope). Hair (12,2) highlight, (12,8) base; facial hair (14,8); brows (12,10) (14,10). Starter_01 hair base `#352A1F`, highlight `#987451`. The HumanSpecies maps put `#FF0000` at (12,2) and are not usable as looks.

**What the review changed from v1**
1. Rows are OPTIONAL, "None" shown as "From look". No choice = the look's own material exactly as today. That removes the blockers at the root: the base-prefab bake (`CharacterRosterAuthor` applies the default look and saves the prefab; a runtime `HideAndDontSave` material would be saved as a missing reference), every saved NPC, and the look 2-4 tests (`HumanAppearanceTests` asserted `m == look.material`).
2. NO `palettePixels` in the catalog (the Synty licence forbids redistributing its textures; `.gitignore` already ignores derived Synty data, and the catalog is committed). The base pixels are read at runtime from the look material's own `_ColorMap`. An `AssetPostprocessor` makes the Sidekick colour maps readable and UNCOMPRESSED (they were BC1: one pixel per cell, so compression shifted the cells; a composed RGBA32 copy would otherwise look different from the untouched look).
3. The nose (2,2) is a skin cell. The skin tones are explicit per-cell colours: main `T`, lighter cells `T + (+26, +26, +20)` clamped (the Starter_01 offset; an ADDITIVE offset instead of a per-channel ratio, which clips and shifts hue for pale tones).
4. The cache key is the look material plus the chosen option ids AND their cell values, so a rebuilt catalog with the same ids but new colours never serves a stale texture. No catalog-specific names in the applier.
5. The new rows are APPENDED after `build` (the randomizer draws per row in order: inserting before `build` would reshuffle it for the same seed).

**Design**
- `ModelAppearanceKind.ColourCells` (appended), `ModelAppearanceCell {x, y, colour}`, `option.cells`, `slot.noneTitle` (the "None" label of an optional row; empty = "None").
- `ModelAppearanceColours.Resolve(look, tints)` (runtime): no tints, no look, or no readable `_ColorMap` (warned once) = the look itself. Else compose the base pixels with the cells in row order into a 32x32 RGBA32 `Texture2D` (sRGB, point, clamp, no mips, `HideAndDontSave`) and `new Material(look)` with `_ColorMap` set and the look's name; cached; destroyed before an assembly reload and cleared on a no-reload play mode entry.
- `ModelAppearanceApplier`: collects the chosen `ColourCells` options in slot order and paints the resolved material through the existing palette step (which runs on every Apply: changing only the skin row repaints). Only part sets take it; Polygon hair, beards and helmets keep their own colours (a Polygon beard does not follow `haircolour`).
- Human catalog: Colour tab rows `skin` (pale, fair, light, tan, bronze, brown, dark, deep) and `haircolour` (black, dark brown, brown, auburn, ginger, blond, grey, white), swatches from the base colour. Family-switch pruning already drops rows another catalog lacks.
- Tests: composed pixels (cells override, every other cell stays the look's), no tint = the look's own material (so the baked base prefab has no runtime material), cache identity and invalidation, Polygon pieces untouched, Sidekick hair takes the colour, graphics render check, a skin-only change repaints, goblin and Viking unchanged, creator "From look" label, PlayMode saved choice; mutation checks.

## 11. Phase C2: relief on the Viking tunic (2026-10-08)

The tunic is the body inflated along unified normals. Relief is added WITHOUT new geometry: the vertices of each band's faces are raised by an extra `RELIEF` amount along the same unified normal (`build_outfit.py`: `categorise` decides every garment face on the ORIGINAL body, `relief_lifts` keys the lift by POSITION across all regions, `inflate` adds `lift * min(1, thickness / 1.5)`). Bands: belt 1.2 cm, collar 1.2, cuff 0.9, hem 1.0, boot fur cuff 1.6, a belt buckle (front-centre belt faces, 6 cm wide, 2.4 cm) in a metal colour cell (18,20). Why vertex lifts and not extrusion: the region meshes are low-poly with irregular rings (torso 292 verts, hips 311) so a lifted band slopes into its neighbours as a bevelled step; a position-keyed lift cannot crack the seams between region meshes; the SAME offset goes into every shape key as before (Buff/Heavy/Skinny/Feminine); topology, vertex groups and UVs are untouched, so every existing outfit test applies unchanged. The lift fades with the existing taper (neck, wrist, sole), so the cuff still meets the hand. `trim` was split into `collar/cuff/hem` (same colour cell) so each stands out by its own amount.

Tests: the existing tunic tests (seam, wrist gap, shape keys on every part, one renderer per region); NEW: the tunic's belt front stands at least 3 cm proud of the bare body (1.5-1.6 cm thickness + buckle) while the plain chest stays under 2.2 cm; the belt, collar, cuff and hem rings each stand proud of the plain tunic next to them.

## 12. Phases B + C1 merged: skinned Polygon pieces fitted to the human (design v1, 2026-10-08, NOT reviewed)

**Measured** (Blender import of `VikingRealm_Characters.fbx`): the baked Viking attachments are skinned to the Polygon rig (50 bones) and are NOT head-only: Hair_01..05 (+ `_Alt`) are 46-83% `Head` and 14-41% `Spine_03` (long hair falls on the back), Wolf_01 70% Head / 23% Spine_03, Bear_01 55% / 32%, Fur_01..04 54-68% `Spine_03` with `Shoulder_L/R` and `Clavicle_L/R` (7-13% each). One material (`BaseMAT`, the Polygon atlas), no shape keys. So the original B plan (bake to a RIGID mesh in the Head frame) loses the spine weighting, and the rigid pieces clip on Buff/Heavy bodies (C1). The two problems have one solution: a Unity EDITOR-time fitter that turns a Polygon piece into a SKINNED part of the Sidekick master skeleton.

**Deviation from the earlier plan, flagged**: C1 was planned as a Blender helper (Surface Deform, shape-key bake, Data Transfer weights). It is built as an editor-time C# tool instead (`PolygonPieceFitter`, `VikingRaid.Appearance.Editor`), because the pieces already live in Unity as meshes with bone weights, the retarget is a few lines of linear algebra, and a C# tool is unit-testable with numeric assertions (clearance, weights, bounds) and needs no FBX round trip (units, avatar and rig import settings were the source of three earlier debugging rounds). Blender stays for the tunic only.

**Retarget (B).** For a source SMR (Polygon master asset, mesh space = the upright rig frame at rest): the source bone rest position is read from the mesh's own bind poses (`p_b = (rendererToWorld * bindpose_b^-1).position`, so the source rest is exactly the pose the mesh was skinned at). For each vertex `v` with weights `(b, w)`: `v' = sum_b w * (P'_b + s * (v - p_b))`, where `P'_b` is the rest position of the mapped Sidekick bone (read from the master PREFAB ASSET like the applier's `RestPose`), `s = 0.935` (Sidekick height / Polygon height, the existing `PolygonScale`). Rotations are NOT applied: both skeletons rest in an upright T pose and the Polygon pieces are modelled in world axes (same reason as the rigid mount rule, local rotation = inverse of the bone's rest rotation), so only positions and one uniform scale differ. Weights are remapped by NAME through a fixed table (Hips pelvis, Spine_01..03 spine_01..03, Neck neck, Head head, Clavicle_L/R clavicle_l/r, Shoulder_L/R upperarm_l/r, Elbow lowerarm, Hand hand; a source bone without a target is merged into its parent chain), then the 4 largest weights are kept and renormalised. Normals and UVs are copied (a translation per bone plus a uniform scale leaves them valid); triangles and sub-meshes are kept. The new mesh gets the master's bind poses (`M_b'^-1` for each used bone), so the applier's `BindPart` finds the master rest equal to the bind pose and creates no bind bones. Output: one mesh asset + one part prefab (the master's skeleton hierarchy with a child SMR bound by bone order) under `Assets/Appearance/SidekickHumans/Pieces/` (derived Synty geometry: ignored by git, like the Blender tunic).

**Fit (C1).** For each body build key (`defaultBuff`, `defaultHeavy`, `defaultSkinny`, `masculineFeminine`) the piece gets a blend shape of the same name (so `ApplyBlendShapes` drives it with the build row, nothing new in the applier). The delta at a piece vertex is the inverse-distance-weighted mean of the body's own blend-shape deltas at its 8 nearest bare-body vertices (all 12 region meshes, basis positions), faded to zero between 2 cm and 8 cm from the surface so a floating tail does not follow. Optional `standoff` (cm) pushes a vertex out along the nearest body normal so a piece worn over the tunic clears it (furs 1.5 cm, hair 0.3, pelts 1.0). Two modes: `Retarget` (above, hairs, wolf, bear, furs: a deforming piece) and `RigidFollow` (a static prefab such as a pouch or bag becomes a part skinned 100% to its mount bone with a UNIFORM-TRANSLATION blend shape per key = the delta at the nearest body vertex to its centroid, so the object keeps its shape and moves out with the body).

**Applier/catalog changes (small).** New option kind `AttachPartsOwnMaterial` (appended to the enum): attached exactly like `AttachParts` (bind by bone name, remove with the slot, `keep` skip) but the part's renderers get `option.material` (the Polygon atlas material) and the palette step does NOT repaint it (the existing rule repaints only `AttachParts`, so nothing changes there). The Polygon texture atlas colours stay as authored (a Viking hair is not recoloured by the Sidekick `haircolour` row: known limit, same as Polygon static hair).

**Options added** (ids reuse the Viking catalog's so a Viking-to-Human switch keeps them): `hair`: Viking skinned hairs 01, 01-alt, 02, 03, 03-alt, 04, 04-alt, 05; `headwear`: wolf pelt, bear pelt; `shoulders`: furs 01-04; `gear`: Viking pouches 01-02 and the War Camp bags, backpacks, bottles and collar as `RigidFollow` (they replace today's rigid `AttachStatic` options of the same ids, so existing saved ids keep working).

**Tests (tests first)**: EditMode: remapped weights only reference bones that exist on the master and sum to 1; fitted vertices stay within the expected region (hair near the head bone); the piece applies with no warning, binds without bind bones (no `_bind` objects), keeps the Polygon material and is NOT repainted by a Look or a skin/hair choice; shape names resolve on the piece mesh and every build weight leaves no residue; CLEARANCE: for every build, no vertex of a fitted piece lies deeper than 0.5 cm inside the bare body (nearest-vertex normal test), and the same numeric test FAILS for the old rigid mount at Heavy (so the test measures something); the rigid-follow pieces keep their size (bounds extents unchanged) under every build; ids unique and the Viking ids are reused; catalog counts; baked base prefab holds only asset materials. PlayMode: a human NPC with a fur, wolf, hair and bag is rebuilt from saved state in the village scene and survives Buff/Heavy. Mutation checks on the retarget (scale, bone map, weights), the fit (key names, fade), the applier (own-material kind repainted).

**Questions for the review**: (a) is the rest-position-only retarget sound (no rotation), what breaks if a Polygon bone's rest differs in orientation from the Sidekick's (is the Polygon T pose world-aligned like the Sidekick's?), (b) `Mesh` APIs in the EDITOR on the Synty meshes (is `isReadable` false and does `GetAllBoneWeights`/`vertices`/`GetBlendShapeFrameVertices` still work from an editor script), (c) can a generated Mesh asset plus a prefab built by copying the master hierarchy be referenced from the committed catalog without dangling references on a machine that lacks the ignored Synty-derived folder (the committed `HumanAppearanceCatalog.asset` already references the ignored tunic FBXs: how does that fail today and is `AttachPartsOwnMaterial` any worse), (d) normals after a position-dependent weighted blend (non-rigid when two bones' translations differ), (e) the blend-shape fit for a piece far from the body (wolf and bear heads), 8 nearest vertices, the 2 to 8 cm fade, standoff along a low-poly body normal, (f) `RigidFollow` skinned to a single bone with a translation blend shape versus keeping the object a static child: what do builds do to pelvis/spine_03 positions (do the BONES move with the build? if the master skeleton does not move with the blend shape, a static child never follows), (g) whether `updateWhenOffscreen` and bounds are handled for blend-shaped pieces, (h) anything missed (ids, saved NPCs, family-switch pruning for the changed Viking-id options, tests, effort).
